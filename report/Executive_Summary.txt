NASSAU CANDY DISTRIBUTOR
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Objective
Develop a decision-support system that predicts order lead time and evaluates potential factory reassignment scenarios while retaining visibility into historical profitability.

Approach
• Cleaned and validated the supplied Nassau Candy transactional dataset.
• Defined Lead Time = Ship Date − Order Date.
• Engineered calendar and profitability features.
• Compared Linear Regression, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting.
• Evaluated models using MAE, RMSE, and R².
• Built an explainable factory what-if engine using the five supplied factories.
• Used historical gross margin as profitability context.
• Built a Streamlit dashboard for interactive scenario analysis.

Key finding
The initial held-out evaluation showed Random Forest performing better than the two other required models. The final model comparison is saved in models/model_comparison.csv after running the training script.

Decision-support output
For a selected product, region, and ship mode, the dashboard identifies the current factory, estimates baseline lead time, compares all five factory scenarios, reports estimated lead-time improvement, shows historical gross margin, and flags scenarios with limited historical support.

Important limitation
The supplied dataset does not contain historical factory assignments, transportation costs, or factory capacity. Therefore, the system does not claim that the ML model has learned a causal factory effect. Factory reassignment values are scenario estimates based on a transparent geographic proxy. Absolute logistics cost savings and capacity optimization cannot be calculated from the available data.

Recommended business next step
To move from prototype to operational optimization, collect historical factory assignment, shipment-origin data, customer coordinates, freight cost, production capacity, production processing time, and service-level constraints.
